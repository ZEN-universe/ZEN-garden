.. _t_retrofit.t_retrofit:

###############################
Retrofitting and fuel switching
###############################

.. admonition:: At a glance
   :class: note

   | **You will** add a carbon capture unit to an existing power plant, then use the 
        same mechanism to substitute one fuel for another.
   | **You need** the setup from :ref:`tutorials_intro.setup` and
        :ref:`t_add_techs_carriers.t_add_techs_carriers`.

A **retrofitting technology** behaves like an ordinary conversion technology
with one difference: its reference carrier flow is tied to the reference
carrier flow of another technology. It cannot run on its own.

That coupling is the entire point. It is what stops a carbon capture unit from
capturing carbon that no power plant produced, and what stops a fuel-switching
technology from replacing every unit of gas in the system rather than only the
gas burned by one plant.

Every other aspect of retrofitting technologies is the same as for ordinary conversion
technologies: they have the same parameters, the same cost structure, and the
same input files. 

.. _t_retrofit.where:

Where retrofitting technologies live
====================================

Retrofitting technologies are a child class of conversion technologies, and
their folders sit inside a dedicated subfolder:

.. code-block:: text

    set_technologies/
    `--set_conversion_technologies/
       |--CHP_plant/
       |   `--attributes.json
       `--set_retrofitting_technologies/
          |--carbon_capture/
          |   `--attributes.json
          `--e_fuel_production/
              `--attributes.json

They are listed in ``system.json`` under their own set:

.. code-block:: json

    "set_retrofitting_technologies": [
        "carbon_capture"
    ]


.. _t_retrofit.coupling:

The coupling factor
===================

One extra parameter links the retrofit to its base technology:

.. code-block:: json

    "retrofit_flow_coupling_factor": {
      "base_technology": "CHP_plant",
      "default_value": 0.18,
      "unit": "kilotons/GWh"
    }

It names the base technology and gives the ratio between the reference carrier
flow of the retrofit and that of the base technology. The constraint is an
inequality, i.e., the retrofit flow must be **less than or equal to** the base flow
times the factor, which is what permits *partial* retrofitting.

.. important::
    The factor is expressed per unit of the **base technology's reference
    carrier**, not per unit of fuel. For a carbon capture unit on a CHP plant
    whose reference carrier is electricity, ``0.18 kilotons/GWh`` means up to
    0.18 kilotons of carbon captured per GWh of electricity generated. Getting
    this ratio the wrong way round is the most common mistake in this tutorial.


.. _t_retrofit.ccs:

Application 1: retrofitting with carbon capture
===============================================

The straightforward case. A gas power plant consumes gas and produces
electricity; the capture unit consumes electricity and produces captured
carbon. The capture unit's reference carrier is carbon, so the coupling factor
is carbon captured per unit of electricity generated by the plant.

The captured carbon then needs somewhere to go, for example, an ordinary
conversion technology such as ``carbon_storage`` that consumes carbon
permanently, a technology that produces synthetic fuels by consuming carbon,
or exporting carbon to get an emission credit.


.. _t_retrofit.fuel_switch:

Application 2: fuel switching
=============================

Less intuitively, the same mechanism handles fuel substitution.

Suppose you want to replace part of the natural gas burned by a gas turbine
with biogenic gas. Rather than defining a new turbine that burns both at a
fixed ratio, define a retrofitting technology that **produces natural gas** and
**consumes biogenic gas** at a ratio of 1, coupled to the turbine.

Why go through the retrofit class at all? Because a plain conversion technology
that turns biogas into natural gas could substitute *all* the natural gas
anywhere in the system. Coupling it to the turbine limits the substitution to
the gas that turbine actually burns — which is what "switching this plant's
fuel" means.


Exercises
=========

The exercises are cumulative. Use the dedicated example dataset:

.. code-block:: shell

    zen-example --dataset="14_retrofitting_and_fuel_substitution"
    zen-garden --dataset="14_retrofitting_and_fuel_substitution"

This dataset adds a ``CHP_plant``, a ``carbon_capture`` retrofit, a
``carbon_storage`` technology and an ``e_fuel_production`` retrofit to the
familiar two-node system.

.. note::
    The shipped dataset already has a carbon price ramping 100/200/300
    Euro/ton over 2023-2025, plus a hard annual emissions limit of 0 in 2025
    (``energy_system/carbon_emissions_annual_limit.csv``). Both are already
    pushing the system towards capture and towards zero emissions in the last
    year. Check ``energy_system/attributes.json`` and the accompanying
    ``.csv`` files before you start, the same way
    :ref:`t_emissions.t_emissions` recommends for the plain emissions
    tutorial.

1. **Confirm the coupling actually binds.** Read the output carrier flows of
   the CHP plant and the carbon capture unit 
   (``r.get_full_ts("flow_conversion_output", index=["carbon_capture","CHP_plant"])``), 
   and check the ratio between them
   against ``retrofit_flow_coupling_factor``.

   *Expected result: captured carbon never exceeds the CHP electricity output
   times the coupling factor.*

   *On this dataset: The reference carrier of the CHP plant is electricity, 
   and in base units (megatons and GWh) the coupling factor is 0.00018.
   In time steps where the CHP plant is running (output > 1e-7), 
   the ratio of captured carbon to electricity is at 0.00018.*

3. **Break the coupling on purpose, to see what it was doing.** Copy the
   dataset and move ``carbon_capture`` out of
   ``set_retrofitting_technologies`` into ``set_conversion_technologies``,
   removing its ``retrofit_flow_coupling_factor`` and listing it under
   ``set_conversion_technologies`` in ``system.json``. To avoid an unbounded 
   solution, set the capacity limit of the capture unit to 100 kiloton/hour in 
   ``set_technologies/carbon_capture/attributes.json``. Re-run the model.

   *Expected result: the carbon capture plant constantly runs at its capacity limit, and
   captures carbon, regardless of the CHP plant's operation. In this example, it is
   incentivized to do so because the carbon price is high and reimburses the
   costs of capture.*
